# Clause explanation checks

## Preserve the source meaning

For each clause, check these fields against the actual wording:

| Field                      | Questions                                                                      |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Actor and modality         | Who must, may or must not do something? Is the obligation mutual or one-sided? |
| Action and object          | What is required, permitted, licensed, assigned or restricted?                 |
| Trigger and timing         | What starts the duty, which dates/time units apply, and is notice required?    |
| Conditions and exceptions  | Which qualifiers, carve-outs, thresholds or dependencies change the rule?      |
| Limits and amounts         | Which currency, fee base, period, cap or exclusion is actually stated?         |
| Definitions and references | Which defined terms and other provisions must be read with it?                 |

Keep a minimal exact excerpt and source locator next to the explanation. Do not
silently remove "unless", "except", "may", a negation or a cross-reference to
make a sentence shorter. Preserve operative information in a signature block,
order form or table when present.

## Useful flags

Use an observation rather than an accusation:

- **Material obligation:** identify a concrete action, cost, restriction or deadline.
- **Dependency:** identify a definition, schedule or incorporated term needed to understand it.
- **Exception or scope:** identify a stated qualification that changes the simple reading.
- **Text conflict:** quote the provisions that appear inconsistent; do not invent a precedence rule.
- **Meaning unresolved:** explain which word or relationship supports multiple readings.

Do not attribute intent or bad faith solely from complexity. An unfamiliar clause
is not automatically invalid, unusual in its industry or contrary to law.

## Readability and comprehension

Write for the user's actual audience. Use common words and direct structure while
retaining technical terms needed for fidelity. If an audience review actually
occurs, record the questions asked and observed misunderstandings rather than
claiming that a clear-looking paragraph is proven understandable.

Only report a numerical readability score when an actual method was used. Record
the formula/tool/version, exact sample or coverage, and result. Formula scores
measure selected textual features and do not establish comprehension or accuracy.
If no measurement ran, write unmeasured instead of estimating an exact grade.

## Final report review

Check quotations, actor mapping, deadlines/units, exceptions, dependencies, summary
traceability and coverage limits. A plain-language explanation is not substitute
contract wording unless a separate drafting task explicitly requests that work.
Do not edit originals, send notices or accept terms as part of explaining them.

## Primary guidance

Reviewed 2026-10-25; these are writing/workflow sources, not rules determining a
private contract's legal effect.

- [Digital.gov plain-language guides](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language):
  audience-focused writing, design and testing.
- [Indian Health Service: measuring readability](https://www.ihs.gov/healthcommunications/plain-language/measuring-readability/):
  readability formulas are only one part of evaluating written communication.
- [Florida Bar Opinion 24-1](https://www.floridabar.org/etopinions/opinion-24-1/):
  advisory guidance on confidentiality, professional judgment and verification of
  AI-assisted work. It does not impose a universal rule on every report reader.
