# Proposal and source checks

## Replacement language

Work from the actual business objective and clause context. Keep actor, obligation,
trigger, exception, amount/unit, timing, remedy, definition and cross-reference
consistent. Check how the proposal interacts with payment, termination, liability,
indemnity, ownership, confidentiality and precedence provisions elsewhere.

Use explicit unresolved parameters for decisions not yet made. Label proposed
commercial numbers as proposals and give their actual basis if supplied. Separate
an opening request, fallback, user-approved limit and counterparty acceptance.
No draft should silently imply the user accepted new performance obligations,
concessions, rights transfers or dispute mechanisms.

## Template comparison

A credible comparison records the actual source title, URL or supplied path,
version/date, section, inspected passage and its relevant context. Check available
license and attribution requirements for the actual material used. Standard terms,
cover pages, explanatory guidance and website copy may have different terms.
Do not infer a contract's licensing from a software repository license alone.

Distinguish unchanged quoted material, adaptations and original drafting. Identify
substantive deviations from a named standard; do not call a modified document an
unchanged standard. Cite only inspected material, and do not claim every provision
in a template is a legal requirement or empirically established market practice.
A template's suitability and enforceability remain separate questions.

Potential starting points when the user supplies accessible copies or research
is separately available:

- [CommonPaper standards](https://commonpaper.com/standards/)
- [Bonterms standard agreements](https://bonterms.com/standard-agreements/)

These links identify possible sources, not evidence that they were read in a
particular negotiation. With this skill's current toolset, use supplied accessible
material or record the missing research; do not claim online verification.

## Evidence and communication

Attach issue IDs to proposals and email references so the other party can locate
the exact change. Distinguish a factual drafting inconsistency from a commercial
request and an unresolved legal question. Explain the substantive effect plainly.
Do not cite fictitious counsel approval, industry consensus, counterparty motives
or a predicted acceptance rate. Multiple model opinions are not market evidence.

Present the email as an unsent draft. A request for a negotiation plan does not
send it, book a meeting, file a document, alter the agreement or execute a signature.
