# Comparison report and evidence review

## Document and coverage record

Record the selected matter label, report date, baseline and revision identifiers,
source paths and which party's perspective is being considered. Reuse supplied
version labels; do not invent file hashes or signatures. Record hashes only when
actually computed with an available authorized tool.

List the pages/sections/exhibits read and each known gap. Note whether the visible
text is clean, tracked-change markup, a rendered PDF or an extraction. OCR errors,
missing tables, hidden annotations, signatures and unavailable incorporated terms
can limit the result. Normalization must not drop amounts, negations or defined
term capitalization that may affect the comparison.

## Clause map

| Baseline locator    | Revision locator                  | Alignment status                          | Evidence or limit                                  |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Actual section/page | Actual corresponding section/page | Aligned, moved, new, absent or unresolved | Text used for mapping, or why mapping is uncertain |

Use absent/removal only within established coverage. Do not double-count a moved
clause as one deletion plus one addition. A relocated clause can also have a
wording change; show both aspects without inflating the finding count.

## Finding record

For each finding, include:

- **ID and type:** stable identifier plus added/removed/modified/moved.
- **Locations:** original and revised section/page, with exact version labels.
- **Evidence:** minimal exact excerpts; clearly identify omitted material and keep
  quotation boundaries honest. Preserve the documents unchanged.
- **Observation:** the actual textual change, separate from interpretation.
- **Dependencies:** defined terms, cross-references, caps, exceptions, schedules or
  precedence provisions inspected, and any unavailable dependency.
- **Possible effect:** who may benefit or incur an obligation under stated
  assumptions; use neutral, unclear or context-dependent when appropriate.
- **Priority and next question:** tied to the user's stated objective; identify the
  context or legal review needed before deciding what to negotiate.

The report may group presentation-only edits, but must not hide a material wording
change inside that group. Do not infer intentional concealment from a small edit,
renumbering or an expanded definition.

## Counts and conclusions

If reporting counts, reconcile them with finding IDs and explain how moves and
multiple changes in one clause were counted. Do not turn counts or subjective
severity weights into legal safety, likely damages or negotiation success.
One material change can outweigh many cosmetic edits.

A term's absence from one document is a textual observation. Any assertion about
rights, enforceability or default rules requires applicable current law and full
contract context. Do not present a generic UCC, common-law or template reference
as a rule governing every supplied contract. Frame unsupported legal questions for
qualified review instead of inventing citations or conclusions.

## Final review

Check baseline direction and party mapping, both locators and each quotation,
coverage gaps, material dependencies, counts and priority rationale. Verify the
selected output path and preserve unrelated reports. A report draft is not a
contract amendment, legal opinion or authorization to send messages or accept terms.

## Primary workflow guidance

Reviewed 2026-10-25. These sources address professional use of AI and output review;
they do not determine the interpretation of the user's contract or create a
universal jurisdictional rule.

- [Florida Bar Opinion 24-1](https://www.floridabar.org/etopinions/opinion-24-1/):
  advisory guidance emphasizes confidentiality, independent professional judgment
  and verification of AI-assisted work.
- [ABA announcement of Formal Opinion 512](https://www.americanbar.org/news/abanews/aba-news-archives/2024/07/aba-issues-first-ethics-guidance-ai-tools/):
  discusses competence, confidentiality and review responsibilities when lawyers
  use generative AI.
